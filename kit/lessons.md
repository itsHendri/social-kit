# Lessons

What was learned running a setup like this one. Setup offers these as defaults. The owner keeps what fits and drops the rest.

## Planning
- A weekly number per channel works best as a ceiling, not a target. Leave a slot empty rather than pad it with a weak post, and say how many slots are still open.
- Don't put two posts on one channel on the same day.
- Spread posts across the week, using the channel's times in order.
- Plan from the media that exists. Ask only what the files can't tell you (what a screen recording shows, which launch a photo belongs to). If it's clear, don't ask.
- When a vertical and a landscape version of the same video exist, make one post per version, on different channels or days. Never post the same video twice to one channel.
- Where several versions of a file exist (`-v01`, `-v02`), use the newest.
- Copy renders out of a video project, don't move them, so the project keeps its outputs.
- Anything in `incoming/` that couldn't be used stays there, with a line saying why.

## Formats that usually fit
Confirm each one with the owner in the channel round.
- 9:16 video: Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok.
- 16:9 video: YouTube, LinkedIn.
- Images and carousels: Instagram, LinkedIn.
- A horizontal video cropped to vertical usually does badly. Reframe it, or make a vertical version on purpose.

## Files
- Lowercase kebab-case file names, dates first: `2026-12-01-studio-tour`.
- On a Mac, convert iPhone photos from HEIC to JPG with `sips -s format jpeg in.heic --out out.jpg`.
- In a `post.md` header, put quotes around any value with ` #` in it. Without them, everything after the `#` is dropped.
- Keep big video files out of git. Git keeps every version forever, and the folder gets slow to copy and back up.

## Getting better over time
- When the owner rewrites a draft, the reason is the useful part. Write it into `voice.md` or the channel file, not just into that one post.
- Judge posts by the outcome the owner named in their goals (enquiries, sales, subscribers), not by views alone.
- Write each learning down with a status: `active` (do this), `watch` (seen once or twice, keep an eye on it), `retired` (stopped being true).

## Writing that reads as AI
Offer this list in the voice round. Keep only what the owner picks.
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "The problem isn't X. The problem is Y."
- Lists of three in every post.
- Setting up a point, making it, then saying it again at the end.
- Asking a question and answering it straight away ("The secret? Consistency.").
- Stock openers: "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "In today's world", "Unlock".
- Em dashes everywhere.
- One sentence per line for a whole post.
- Engagement bait at the end: "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "Comment YES".
- Vulnerability that sounds made up for the post.
- Emoji used as bullet points.
- Claims with no proof: a post about results needs a real result.
- Talking down to the reader ("You've been doing this wrong").

## Hook types
Offer these in the voice round. The owner picks the ones that sound like them. Every hook needs a real detail of theirs in it (a number, a name, a moment), or it reads as a template.
- Curiosity: something surprising, or "I was wrong about X".
- Story: a moment, a near miss, a before and after.
- Useful: a how-to, a short list, "stop doing X, do Y".
- Opinion: a view most people in the field don't share.
- Proof: a result, or a question someone asked.
- Choice: two real options side by side, and which one wins.
